# 9.6 — Subscription & Email Settings

> Boring dashboard toggles with real-money consequences. Treat them like code.

## Why this matters

Four of the five settings we're about to configure live only in Stripe's dashboard. They're not in your repo, they're not in your migrations, they're not in CI. That makes them easy to set once, forget, and later discover that production has a different configuration than test mode — usually at the moment a customer emails "I never got a receipt" or "my card failed three months ago and you just cancelled me."

This lesson covers what to set, why, and — most importantly — how to *version-control the intent* of these settings via a small runbook file. When a new engineer joins Contactly, they shouldn't have to reverse-engineer the production Stripe settings from customer complaints.

## The Principal Engineer lens

**Infrastructure you can't `git blame` will surprise you.** Dashboard settings are configuration. They affect production behaviour. They deserve the same discipline as code: a documented source of truth, a diff when they change, a review before they change. We can't (yet) enforce that via automation, but we can write it down.

Corollary: **the deepest surprises come from the settings you never touched.** Stripe ships reasonable defaults. Those defaults change. A 2022 Stripe account and a 2026 Stripe account start with different customer-email defaults. Don't trust "I didn't change it" — explicitly set and document every setting we rely on.

## Step 1 — Customer emails

Stripe can send emails to your customers on your behalf. Found at **Settings → Emails** in the dashboard.

Three sections matter. Our recommended config:

| Setting | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Email customers about successful payments | **Enabled** | A receipt per renewal is table stakes. We don't (yet) send our own. |
| Email customers about failed payments | **Enabled** | Reinforces the in-app `past_due` banner from 9.5. Redundancy is fine. |
| Email customers about expiring cards | **Enabled** | Proactive: Stripe notices card expiry and emails 30 days out. We'd have to build this ourselves otherwise. |

Enabling these means Stripe sends branded emails with your logo and support email (configured under **Settings → Public details**). Set those to `hello@contactly.app` and your real logo before enabling — an unbranded "Stripe" receipt erodes trust.

**Don't disable the successful-payment email** until you've built your own. "Users get nothing after paying" is worse than "users get two emails after paying." Module 12 introduces a transactional email provider (Resend) and then we revisit.

## Step 2 — Subscription retry & dunning

Found at **Settings → Subscriptions and emails → Manage failed payments**. This is the retry schedule from 9.5.

Stripe offers three levers:

- **Retry schedule.** How many attempts, spaced over how many days. Default is typically 4 attempts over 3 weeks.
- **What happens on final failure.** Cancel the subscription, or leave it `unpaid` (user has to manually reactivate).
- **Whether to email the customer between retries.** Yes, at minimum after the first failure.

Contactly's choice:

- **3 retries over 2 weeks.** Tight enough to be decisive, long enough to cover payday variance (Stripe's "Smart Retries" ML setting also works fine; manual is predictable).
- **Cancel on final failure.** Simpler than `unpaid`; user has to re-checkout, which keeps our data model clean (new sub = new row, not a resurrected one).
- **Email on first failure and on cancellation.** Two emails is the minimum respectful amount.

## Step 3 — Customer Portal

Found at **Settings → Billing → Customer portal**. This is the hosted subscription-management UI we'll deliver in 9.7 and 9.8. Touch it now only to:

- Set the business name and logo (shared with the receipt emails).
- Confirm the "Products" section has both Plus and Pro listed, monthly + yearly. Without them, users can't change plans. (9.7 does this in detail.)

Full configuration lives in 9.7; we note it here so the runbook lists every billing-adjacent setting in one place.

## Step 4 — Public details and branding

Found at **Settings → Public details** and **Settings → Branding**.

- **Business name** — "Contactly" (not your LLC name, unless they're the same).
- **Support email** — `hello@contactly.app`. This appears in receipt emails and the Portal.
- **Statement descriptor** — 22-char string that appears on the customer's bank statement. `CONTACTLY APP` is fine; avoid underscores (some banks render them as spaces).
- **Logo** — PNG on a transparent background, roughly 128×128. Uploaded once, used everywhere.

These look cosmetic; they're not. A customer who sees `SQUARE*UNKNOWNMERCHANT` on their statement will dispute the charge. A clear descriptor + matching email = fewer disputes.

## Step 5 — API version pinning (dashboard side)

Found at **Developers → API**. Our code pins `apiVersion: '2026-03-25.dahlia'` (Module 6.1). The dashboard also has a "Default API version" setting for your account, which controls the version used by:

- Dashboard-initiated actions (e.g., a human clicking "Issue refund").
- Webhooks, *if* the webhook endpoint doesn't explicitly pin a version.

Our webhook endpoint in the dashboard (configured separately, below) should pin the same version. When they drift, you see bugs only on dashboard-initiated actions, which is maddening to track down.

## Step 6 — Webhook endpoint in the dashboard

Found at **Developers → Webhooks**. You already created one for `stripe listen` forwarding in local dev; production will have another pointing at your deployed URL (Module 12.5 walks through this). For each endpoint:

- **API version** — pin to `2026-03-25.dahlia`. Don't leave it on "Your account's default."
- **Events to send** — the minimum set your handler cares about:
  - `checkout.session.completed`
  - `customer.subscription.created`
  - `customer.subscription.updated`
  - `customer.subscription.deleted`
  - `customer.subscription.trial_will_end`
  - `invoice.paid`
  - `invoice.payment_failed`
  - `customer.created` (7.3 uses this)
- **Signing secret** — stored in `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` (Module 6.1). Different per endpoint.

"Send all events" works but doubles your log volume for no gain. Pick the set you handle.

## Step 7 — The runbook

Every setting above should live in a single markdown file in the repo. Create `docs/stripe-settings.md`:

```md
# Stripe Account Settings — Runbook

> Canonical reference for the dashboard-only configuration that production depends on.
> If this file is out of sync with either the test or live Stripe account, production is at risk.

## Accounts

- **Test mode** — `acct_1TestXxx...` (shared across all engineers).
- **Live mode** — `acct_1LiveXxx...` (production-only; access-controlled).

Every setting below must match across test and live.

## Settings → Public details

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Business name | Contactly |
| Support email | hello@contactly.app |
| Support phone | (none) |
| Statement descriptor | CONTACTLY APP |

## Settings → Branding

- Logo: `brand/logo-128.png` (in repo at `app/static/brand/`).
- Brand color: `#6366F1` (matches Tailwind `primary`).

## Settings → Emails

| Email | State |
|---|---|
| Successful payments | ON |
| Failed payments | ON |
| Expiring cards | ON |

## Settings → Subscriptions → Manage failed payments

- Retry schedule: 3 retries, days 1 / 3 / 7 after initial failure.
- On final failure: Cancel subscription.
- Customer emails: ON after first failure, ON on cancellation.

## Settings → Billing → Customer portal

See `./stripe-customer-portal.md` for the full portal config. Summary:
- Products: Plus (monthly + yearly), Pro (monthly + yearly).
- Features enabled: update payment method, cancel subscription, switch plans, view invoice history.
- Features disabled: billing address capture, custom ToS link, promo codes in-portal.

## Developers → API

- Default API version: `2026-03-25.dahlia` (matches `$lib/server/stripe.ts`).

## Developers → Webhooks

Per endpoint (test + live):
- API version: `2026-03-25.dahlia`
- Events: see `app/src/routes/api/webhooks/stripe/+server.ts` switch.
- Signing secret stored in deployment env as `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`.

## Change log

- 2026-04-19 — Initial runbook (Module 9.6).
```

Two properties of a good runbook:

- **Matches reality.** If you disable an email in the dashboard and don't update this file, the runbook is a lie. Treat edits to the file as part of the PR that changes the setting.
- **Auditable.** A "Change log" section is worth the 30 seconds to maintain. Future-you debugging "when did we enable expiring-card emails?" will thank present-you.

Consider adding a pre-push Git hook (or a CI reminder) that prints "did you update `docs/stripe-settings.md`?" when changes to billing code are staged.

## Step 8 — What our app still handles itself

After all this, Stripe sends:

- Payment receipts.
- Payment-failed emails.
- Expiring-card warnings.

Our app still sends:

- The Supabase Auth welcome email on signup (built in Module 3).
- Eventually (Module 12): in-app transactional emails via a real provider for things Stripe doesn't cover — password reset confirmation, trial-will-end reminders that we want branded our way, etc.

This is the minimum viable email footprint. Most SaaS apps over-email early and then have to unwind it. Start light.

## Step 9 — Keeping test and live in sync

The trap: dev toggles "Successful payments" off in test mode because the email is noisy during development. Six months later, production onboards its first paying customer and never gets a receipt.

Two mitigations:

1. **This runbook**, reviewed on every billing PR.
2. **A quarterly dashboard audit**: open `docs/stripe-settings.md` alongside the Stripe dashboard for each account, verify row by row. 10 minutes, avoids six-figure bugs.

In the long run, Stripe supports some settings via API (`stripe.accounts.update(...)`) and via their "App Store" config-as-code. For most teams, a runbook + discipline is enough. Don't over-engineer this until you have a real driver.

## Verify

- `docs/stripe-settings.md` exists, reviewed, matches the dashboard for both modes.
- Public details + branding: signed-up test user receives a receipt with "Contactly" logo and `hello@contactly.app` as support contact.
- Emails: triggering `payment_intent.succeeded` via the Stripe CLI results in a "Payment receipt" email in Inbucket (if you route Stripe's sender, e.g., `test.event@stripe.com`, to your test inbox) — or at least a success log in the Stripe dashboard's email log.
- Retry schedule: using card `0341`, advancing the test clock reproduces 3 retries over roughly 2 weeks of simulated time, then cancellation.
- Webhook endpoint in the dashboard shows `api_version = "2026-03-25.dahlia"` in its details pane.

## Common traps

- **Leaving API version at "account default" on a webhook endpoint.** Stripe bumps the default next quarter; your handler sees new event shapes; nothing explodes until a subscription-updated payload omits a field you rely on.
- **Turning off customer emails in test, leaving them on in live — and vice versa.** Inconsistency across modes hides bugs (dev sees an email, prod doesn't) or creates them (dev assumes the email is on, prod's off). Same config, both modes.
- **Skipping the statement descriptor.** Generic descriptors (`STRIPE*MYSAAS`) drive disputes. 22 characters; make them count.
- **Putting the support phone number on when you don't have one.** You'll get calls. Leave it blank.
- **Treating the runbook as optional.** It's the single artifact that makes dashboard settings reviewable. Without it, every change is tribal knowledge.
- **Relying on Stripe's default retry schedule without reading it.** "Stripe's defaults" drift. Read, set, document.

## Recap

A small set of Stripe dashboard settings — emails, retries, branding, API version, webhook events — owns a large fraction of production correctness. Written into a runbook, audited quarterly, they stop being tribal knowledge.
