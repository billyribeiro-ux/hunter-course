# 9.7 — Configure Customer Portal

> Don't build a subscription management UI. Stripe already built a better one.

## Why this matters

Every SaaS app needs: update card, change plan, cancel, view past invoices, download receipts. These are not product differentiators; they are four forms and a table that every app implements identically, painfully. Getting them right means handling proration, 3DS reauthentication, retroactive cancellation, dunning resume-on-retry, invoice PDFs, and a long tail of edge cases.

Stripe's **Customer Portal** is a hosted web UI that does all of that. You configure what it can do, generate a per-user session URL on your server, redirect the user to it, and they come back to your app when they're done. This lesson configures the dashboard side; Lesson 9.8 wires up the server-side delivery.

## The Principal Engineer lens

**Don't reinvent subscription management UI.** The Customer Portal is ~a thousand engineer-hours of correct edge-case handling, translations, accessibility, and ongoing compliance, delivered free. You could write a "just the card update flow" page in a week. It'd be worse. You'd then maintain it forever.

Corollary: **the Portal's capabilities are set dashboard-side; your app only decides *who* gets to use it.** You don't selectively disable "cancel subscription" for some users in code — if you offer cancellation, you offer it to everyone. The Portal's configuration is a product decision, not a runtime one.

## Step 1 — Open the configuration page

In the Stripe dashboard: **Settings → Billing → Customer portal → Configure**.

You'll see a preview on the right and a sidebar of feature toggles on the left. Toggle changes auto-save and auto-reflect in the preview. The same configuration is used for every portal session your app creates in this Stripe account + mode.

## Step 2 — Business information

Top section. Most of this is inherited from **Settings → Public details** (Module 9.6) but you can override here:

- **Business name** — Contactly.
- **Privacy policy** — link to `https://contactly.app/privacy`.
- **Terms of service** — link to `https://contactly.app/terms`.

Both legal links are required by Stripe for the Portal to be enabled. If you don't have policies yet, write them — a page-long plain-English doc per topic is far better than no page at all.

## Step 3 — Features: what to enable

The sidebar has a list of capabilities. Contactly's chosen configuration:

| Feature | State | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Invoice history** | ON | Users want receipts and PDFs. Zero app code. |
| **Customer information** | OFF for billing address, email changes | Email is managed in `/account`. Billing address we don't collect. |
| **Payment methods** | ON (add + remove + set default) | Core failed-payment recovery path. |
| **Cancel subscriptions** | ON, at end of billing period | Default; we don't want immediate cancellation without deliberate choice. |
| **Pause subscriptions** | OFF | Not a feature we offer. Adds decision paralysis. |
| **Update subscriptions** | ON — allow plan change across Plus ↔ Pro and monthly ↔ yearly | The upgrade/downgrade path. |
| **Promotion codes** | OFF in-portal | Promo codes are an acquisition tool, not a retention one. |

A few decisions worth expanding.

## Step 4 — Cancellations: end-of-period vs. immediate

When a user clicks "Cancel subscription," the Portal can either:

- **Cancel at end of billing period** (default). `cancel_at_period_end = true`. User keeps access until the period ends; Stripe stops charging after.
- **Cancel immediately with prorated refund**. User loses access now; Stripe refunds the unused portion.
- **Cancel immediately without refund**. User loses access now; no money back.

Pick end-of-period. Immediate cancellation is user-hostile (they paid for the month), and immediate-with-refund has dispute-risk and cashflow-noise that's not worth it at your stage. End-of-period is what every SaaS does; Stripe agrees in the default.

You can also enable **cancellation reason collection** (radio options: too expensive, missing feature, found alternative, etc.). Turn it on. These responses are visible in the Stripe dashboard's "Cancellations" report and directly inform your next roadmap.

## Step 5 — Plan changes: the Products section

The most error-prone toggle. Under **Update subscriptions → Products**, you explicitly list which products a user can switch to from within the Portal.

- **Required setup:** add Plus (monthly + yearly) and Pro (monthly + yearly). Four prices total.
- **Important:** if you forget to add a product here, users on the Portal will see "No plans available to change to." They'll think cancellation is the only option.

When adding, the dashboard asks "Which prices for this product?" Pick both the monthly and the yearly price for each of Plus and Pro. The Portal will display them as a plan-selection UI with the right toggles.

### Proration

For plan changes, the Portal lets Stripe handle proration automatically:

- **Upgrade Plus → Pro mid-period.** Stripe credits the unused Plus amount, charges the Pro prorated difference, new period starts now. Next renewal is the Pro price.
- **Downgrade Pro → Plus mid-period.** Stripe either credits the difference to the next invoice (default) or issues a refund — configurable.

Leave proration on its defaults. "Always create prorations" is what users expect and what your accounting will thank you for.

## Step 6 — Features to deliberately disable

Each toggle-off is a decision:

- **Billing address capture.** We don't charge tax by jurisdiction (yet — Module 13 discusses Stripe Tax). Collecting addresses we don't use is a privacy liability.
- **Custom Terms of Service link inside subscription flows.** The Portal already shows your ToS from Step 2; a second link per flow is clutter.
- **In-portal promotion codes.** The codes field on the Portal lets users type in any active promo code. Useful *if* your promos are durable and customer-service friendly; dangerous if you run "NEWSIGNUP50OFF" style acquisition promos and don't want existing users stacking them. Contactly keeps promos for the pricing page Checkout only.
- **Pause subscriptions.** Pause is useful for seasonal products. Contactly isn't seasonal. Leaving it off simplifies the UI and eliminates a state (`paused`) we'd otherwise have to handle in our subscription service.

## Step 7 — The "Test the live portal" preview

At the top of the configure page, Stripe provides a **"Preview"** or **"Test the live portal"** button. Click it. Stripe generates a temporary portal session for a synthetic customer (no subscription, or a test one you've created) and opens it in a new tab.

Walk every flow:

1. Click **Update payment method** — card form renders.
2. Click **Cancel subscription** — you see the cancellation reason options (if enabled) and the "cancel at period end" language.
3. Click **Change plan** — the plan selector shows Plus and Pro with both intervals.
4. Click **View invoice history** — if the test customer has invoices, they appear with PDF download links.

If any of these fails or is missing, fix it here before you ship the `/app/billing/portal` route. The worst time to discover "plan change isn't wired up" is when a user tries it.

## Step 8 — Mobile and branding

The Portal inherits your logo and colours from **Settings → Branding** (Module 9.6). It's responsive by default. Don't try to tweak its CSS — you can't, and the default is more accessible than anything you'd build in a day.

On the preview, resize the browser to a mobile viewport. Verify the flows still work. Stripe's Portal is continuously tested across devices; your concern is only that your copy and branding hold up.

## Step 9 — Configuration lives per-mode

As with every Stripe dashboard setting:

- **Test mode** Portal configuration is used when you generate portal sessions with the test secret key.
- **Live mode** has its own configuration.

Configure test first. Verify the flows. Then replicate in live when you're ready for production (Module 12.5 covers this as part of the production checklist).

The runbook from 9.6 (`docs/stripe-settings.md`) should already reference a sibling `docs/stripe-customer-portal.md` with the full per-feature state table. If it doesn't, add it now:

## Step 10 — What the Portal doesn't do

Worth knowing so you don't go looking in vain:

- **It doesn't handle plan suspensions beyond Stripe's pause feature.** If you want "suspend for 30 days without cancelling," you build that.
- **It doesn't expose your custom metadata.** If you want users to see "Next billing: 25 contacts used" that's your app's job, linked *from* the Portal.
- **It doesn't send your emails.** Emails related to actions taken in the Portal (cancellation confirmation, etc.) come from Stripe's email system (Module 9.6's settings), not your app.
- **It doesn't replace `/account`.** Supabase Auth changes (email, password, account delete) stay in your app. The Portal is billing-only.

A clean split: your app owns identity; Stripe owns billing. The Portal lives at that seam.

## Verify

- Stripe dashboard → Settings → Billing → Customer portal → Configure shows the enabled features from Step 3.
- The "Test the live portal" preview loads, and every enabled flow works end-to-end with a test customer.
- Under Update subscriptions → Products, both Plus prices (monthly + yearly) and both Pro prices are listed.
- `docs/stripe-customer-portal.md` exists and matches the dashboard.
- Resizing the preview to mobile: all flows still usable.

## Common traps

- **Enabling plan change but forgetting to list products.** "No plans available to change to." is the user-facing symptom. Always add every tier's prices to the Products section.
- **Allowing cancellations immediately-with-refund by default.** Cashflow-noisy, dispute-risky, user-hostile (they paid for the whole month). Stick with end-of-period.
- **Leaving "Customer information → email" ON.** Users change the email in the Portal; it updates Stripe's Customer.email but not your Supabase `auth.users.email`. Your records drift. Keep email changes in `/account`.
- **Forgetting to configure live mode.** Test works; production has the default (empty) Portal. First production user clicks "Manage" and sees a broken config. Module 12.5 catches this if you follow the checklist.
- **Customising the Portal CSS.** You can't. Attempts to CSS-hack via embedded pages will break at next Stripe deploy. Use the Portal as-is; put custom stuff around it in your app.
- **Assuming the Portal replaces receipts.** It shows invoices. Receipts still come from Stripe email (9.6). Both settings matter.
