# 13.3 — Stripe Branding

> Stripe Checkout and the Customer Portal are owned real estate for your brand. Thirty minutes of config makes the experience feel like Contactly, not Stripe.

## Why this matters

Your user clicks "Upgrade to Plus." The URL changes to `checkout.stripe.com`. They enter card details on a page that… looks generic. Big Stripe logo at the bottom. Default blue buttons. No sign they're still buying *your* product.

That moment of visual whiplash is where trust leaks. Users pause. They wonder if they're being phished. Some abandon checkout entirely. The fix is twenty minutes of settings changes — and Stripe does all the rendering for you.

This lesson makes every Stripe-hosted page look like Contactly: same logo, same accent colour, same typography (to the extent Stripe allows). Same trust.

## The Principal Engineer lens

**The brand is the product's promise. Every surface the user touches should honour it.** Even surfaces you don't own — Stripe Checkout, emailed receipts, the Customer Portal. Especially those, because the user's reasonable assumption is that anything with your product's name on it reflects your quality bar.

Corollary: **the defaults are never the final answer.** Every SaaS company uses Stripe; the ones that bother to customise the branded pages look 10x more serious than the ones that don't. The work is trivial; the signal is loud.

## Step 1 — Prepare your assets

You need three files:

- **Logo**: SVG or PNG with transparent background. 512×128 minimum, aspect ratio around 4:1 works best. Stripe caps at 512×512 but wide logos look cleaner in their header layout.
- **Icon**: square 512×512 PNG. Used as favicon on Stripe-hosted pages and in some emails. A mark, not the wordmark.
- **Brand colour hex**: one primary accent. Contactly's is `#6366F1` (indigo-500). Whatever yours is, write it down.

If you don't have a logo yet, a wordmark in a clean sans-serif (Inter, Söhne, SF Pro) is fine. This is not the lesson to spend a week on logo design.

## Step 2 — Upload to Stripe (live mode)

Stripe dashboard → make sure you're in **live mode** (top-right toggle) → **Settings → Business → Branding**.

- **Brand settings**:
  - **Icon**: upload the 512×512.
  - **Logo**: upload the wide logo.
  - **Brand colour**: hex code.
  - **Accent colour**: for hover states; usually 20% darker than brand colour. Contactly uses `#4F46E5` (indigo-600).
- **Public details** (used on receipts and in Stripe-hosted pages):
  - **Business name**: `Contactly`.
  - **Support email**: `support@contactly.app`.
  - **Support phone**: optional; skip unless you actually answer it.
  - **Statement descriptor**: the 22-character string that appears on credit-card statements. Default is your business name truncated. Prefer something like `CONTACTLY` or `CONTACTLY.APP` — users Google it when they see an unfamiliar charge.

Save. Repeat the upload in **test mode** so your dev/preview checkouts match what customers see.

## Step 3 — Verify on Checkout

Open `/pricing` on prod, click Upgrade to Plus. On the Stripe-hosted page you should see:

- Your logo in the header.
- Your brand colour on the "Pay" button.
- Your support email in the footer.
- No "powered by Stripe" visual prominence — it's still there (legally must be), just not dominant.

If the page still looks default: hard-refresh. Stripe caches branding aggressively; it can take a minute to propagate.

## Step 4 — Customer Portal branding

Stripe dashboard → **Settings → Billing → Customer Portal → Configure**.

- **Business information** tab:
  - **Headline**: "Manage your Contactly subscription" (short, scannable).
  - **Privacy policy URL**: `https://contactly.app/privacy`.
  - **Terms URL**: `https://contactly.app/terms`.
- **Features** tab: already configured in Module 9.7.
- **Appearance** tab: the branding from Step 2 flows through automatically. Nothing extra to set.

## Step 5 — Email receipts

Stripe → **Settings → Emails**.

- **Email settings**:
  - **From name**: `Contactly Billing`.
  - **Reply-to**: `support@contactly.app`.
- **Email templates**: Stripe provides defaults for "Successful payment" and "Failed payment." You can't rewrite them heavily (Stripe controls the structure for compliance reasons), but:
  - The header logo is already picked up from Step 2.
  - You *can* add a footer note: "Questions? Reply to this email or visit contactly.app/help."

If you want heavy customisation, disable Stripe's emails and send your own via Supabase + your SMTP (Module 12.6 Step 7) triggered by webhook events. Not worth it for the first version.

## Step 6 — The checkout `custom_text` field (optional flair)

When creating a Checkout session, you can pass extra copy that Stripe renders inline. Add it in the checkout helper from Module 9.1:

```ts
// src/lib/server/checkout.ts (excerpt)
const session = await stripe.checkout.sessions.create({
	// ... existing fields ...
	custom_text: {
		submit: {
			message: 'You can cancel anytime from your Contactly account.'
		},
		terms_of_service_acceptance: {
			message: 'By subscribing, you agree to the [Contactly Terms](https://contactly.app/terms).'
		}
	},
	consent_collection: {
		terms_of_service: 'required'
	}
});
```

The first message appears above the "Subscribe" button — a gentle "no lock-in" signal. The second forces a terms checkbox. Together they earn 10% of the trust you'd get from a professionally designed in-app checkout. Cheap; worthwhile.

## Step 7 — Domain for receipts

By default, Stripe sends receipts from `receipts@stripe.com`. That's correct and safe, but it's not *yours*.

Stripe → **Settings → Emails → Custom domain**: add `billing.contactly.app`. Stripe gives you a CNAME to add at your registrar. Once verified, receipts come from `receipts@billing.contactly.app` — branded, delivers better, more memorable.

This is a paid feature on Stripe's newer pricing tiers. Not mandatory, but a strong polish signal once you're paying for Stripe anyway.

## Step 8 — Test everything again

Back to the dev-mode real-card flow from Module 12.5:

1. Go to `/pricing` → Upgrade to Plus → Stripe Checkout opens.
2. The page should show your logo, brand colour, custom text.
3. Pay with your real card.
4. Within seconds an email receipt should arrive — from `Contactly Billing`, with your logo in the header.
5. Return to `/app/account` → click Manage billing → Portal opens with your branding.
6. Refund yourself.

If any step looks default/ugly, redo that step's settings — Stripe caches for ~60 seconds.

## Step 9 — A note on Stripe's new Custom Checkout

Stripe's **Custom Checkout** (beta at time of writing, but stable on many accounts) lets you embed Stripe Elements into your own page for full pixel control — your own form layout, your own CSS. It's more work but gives complete design freedom.

We stay with hosted Checkout in this lesson because the ROI of full custom is poor until you have a strong design system and the traffic to A/B test conversion impacts. Hosted Checkout with branding covers 95% of the perceived-quality gap at 5% of the engineering cost.

If you want the full custom path, the Extras module has a dedicated lesson that builds a Simpler Trading-style cart + embedded payment flow with Stripe Elements.

## Verify

- Live-mode Checkout shows your logo, brand colour, custom text.
- Customer Portal shows your logo and the configured headline.
- A real email receipt lands with "Contactly Billing" as the sender and your logo in the header.
- Statement descriptor on a real card shows a recognisable string like `CONTACTLY.APP`.
- Privacy and Terms links on the Portal go to real pages.

## Common traps

- **Forgetting to upload in both test and live modes.** Your local dev checkout looks default while prod looks branded — or vice versa. Always upload to both.
- **Uploading the logo with a background colour.** If the logo has a solid white background and your Stripe page goes dark mode later, the logo looks like a sticker. Always transparent.
- **Over-long statement descriptor.** 22 characters hard cap. Test with a real card; customers ping support about charges they don't recognise.
- **Privacy/Terms URLs pointing at 404s.** The Customer Portal renders the links; users click them; they 404. Ship the legal pages first, link after.
- **Custom `custom_text` that's too long.** Stripe truncates past ~200 characters and it looks sloppy. One sentence each, max.

## Recap

Thirty minutes of settings. Ten surfaces that used to feel like Stripe now feel like your app. The conversion lift isn't measurable from one data point, but the trust lift is instant and durable.
